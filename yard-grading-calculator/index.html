How this calculator works

Regrading builds a wedge of soil — thin at the house end, thick at the far edge:

extra drop (ft) = (target − current) ÷ 12 wedge volume = ½ × extra drop × width × distance cubic yards = volume ÷ 27 × 1.15 (settling) tons = yards × 1.3 topsoil cap = width × distance × 0.25 ft ÷ 27
extra drop (m) = (target − current) ÷ 100 wedge volume = ½ × extra drop × width × distance × 1.15
⚠️ Estimates only. Keep finished grade 4–6 inches below siding or brick. If the low spot sits far out in the yard (not against the house), split the area into strips and treat the uphill edge of each as its own start point.

Regrading tips

  • Mark the target line first. Stakes and string at the final height beat eyeballing — check with a line level.
  • Fill dirt for the bulk, topsoil on top. Fill compacts and stays; topsoil grows grass. Order both separately.
  • Compact in lifts for deep fills, or just order the 15% extra and let it settle through a season.
  • Pair with a drain for severe low spots. A regrade moves surface water; a french drain handles the water that soaks in. Check the pipe fall with the pipe slope calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How much slope should a yard have for drainage?

The widely used guideline is 6 inches of drop over the first 10 feet from the house — roughly a 5 percent grade. Beyond that first zone, a gentler 1 to 2 percent slope carries water away without creating an obvious hill. Flat spots that hold water after rain are the ones worth regrading.

How much does fill dirt cost?

Bulk fill dirt typically runs 8 to 20 dollars per cubic yard delivered, depending on distance and whether it is screened. Topsoil for the final 2 to 4 inch layer costs more, roughly 15 to 40 dollars per yard. Small projects under about 3 yards are often cheaper with bagged soil from a home center.

Should I use fill dirt or topsoil for regrading?

Both: fill dirt (subsoil, low organic content) for the bulk of the grade because it compacts and stays put, then 2 to 4 inches of topsoil on top for grass. Fill dirt alone grows a poor lawn, and topsoil alone compacts and settles over time. Roughly, order fill dirt for 80 percent of the volume and topsoil for the rest.

Can I add soil right against my foundation?

Only up to a point: keep the finished grade at least 4 to 6 inches below the siding, brick, or sill plate. Soil piled against siding invites rot, termites and moisture wicking into the wall. The goal is soil sloping away from a visibly exposed foundation edge, not burying it.

Do I need to compact fill dirt when regrading?

For shallow regrades under about 6 inches, light raking and settling with water is usually enough. For deeper fills, compact in lifts of 4 to 6 inches with a plate compactor, or order about 15 percent extra to allow for natural settling — this calculator already includes that allowance.
