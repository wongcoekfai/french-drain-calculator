How this calculator works

This uses the rational method — the standard quick estimate for peak runoff on small areas:

Q (CFS) = C × I (in/hr) × A (ft²) ÷ 96.23 Q (GPM) = Q (CFS) × 449 basins = Q (GPM) ÷ grate capacity, rounded up
Q (L/s) = C × I (mm/hr) × A (m²) ÷ 3600 basins = Q (L/s) ÷ grate capacity, rounded up
⚠️ Estimates for planning only. Grate capacity depends on ponding depth, debris and installation — apply a safety margin of 25% or more, and size outlet pipes to keep velocity reasonable (≥2 ft/s self-cleaning).

Placement tips

  • Low points only. Basins collect what flows to them — grade the surface so water actually reaches the grate.
  • Keep runs short. No more than ~50 ft of sheet flow to any one basin; split bigger areas into multiple basins.
  • Sump it. Leave 6 in of sump below the outlet so debris settles instead of clogging the pipe.
  • Pipe it with slope. See the pipe slope calculator, then check gravel needs with the gravel calculator.
  • Call before you dig. In the US dial 811 — free and required.

Frequently asked questions

How do you size a catch basin?

Size a catch basin by peak inflow: multiply drainage area by rainfall intensity and a runoff coefficient (the rational method, Q = C×I×A). A 12×12 inch grate passes roughly 20–30 GPM, a 18×18 inch grate 40–60 GPM, and a 24×24 inch grate 80–100 GPM at typical ponding depths. Match total grate area to peak flow with some safety margin.

How much water can a catch basin handle?

It depends on the grate size and how deep water ponds above it. As a rough guide: a 9×9 inch grate passes about 15 GPM, 12×12 about 25 GPM, 18×18 about 50 GPM, and 24×24 about 90 GPM. Two half-basins or a long linear grate move far more than a single square grate.

Where should catch basins be placed?

Put them at the low points of the paved or graded area, one per roughly 500 to 1,000 square feet of tributary area for typical residential driveways and patios, no more than 50 feet of sheet-flow run to any basin, and where the outlet pipe can maintain slope toward daylight or a dry well.

What is the rational method for runoff?

The rational method estimates peak runoff as Q = C × I × A, where C is the runoff coefficient (how much rain becomes flow), I is rainfall intensity, and A is drainage area. It is the standard quick method for areas under about 200 acres and is what this calculator uses.

How deep should a catch basin be?

Most residential catch basins are 12 to 24 inches deep below the grate. Keep at least 6 inches of sump below the outlet pipe to catch debris, and maintain the outlet pipe slope — 1/4 inch per foot for 3 inch pipe is the common minimum.
