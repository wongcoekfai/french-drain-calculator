How this calculator works

There are two steps: estimate the peak flow with the Rational Method, then find the pipe diameter whose full-flow capacity (by Manning's equation) carries that flow with a safety margin. The result is rounded up to a standard culvert size.

Q = 1.008 × C × i × A [cfs; A in acres, i in in/hr] Q_full = Q ÷ max capacity use [80% default → Q_full = 1.25 Q] Q_full = (1.486/n) × A × R^(2/3) × √S [Manning, full circular pipe] A = πD²/4 , R = D/4 , S = slope (ft/ft) D_min = [ Q_full × n × 4 × 4^(2/3) ÷ (1.486 × π × √S) ]^(3/8)
Q = C × i × A ÷ 360 [m³/s; A in hectares, i in mm/hr] Q_full = Q ÷ max capacity use [80% default → Q_full = 1.25 Q] Q_full = (1/n) × A × R^(2/3) × √S [Manning, full circular pipe] A = πD²/4 , R = D/4 , S = slope (m/m) D_min = [ Q_full × n × 4 × 4^(2/3) ÷ (π × √S) ]^(3/8)

The 1.008 factor in US units converts acre-inches per hour to cubic feet per second (it is often rounded to 1.0). The metric form divides by 360 for the same reason, so both unit systems give the same answer.

⚠️ Planning-level estimate only. This sizes the barrel from open-channel full-flow capacity. A real culvert design must also check inlet control and outlet control headwater, tailwater, barrel length, inlet geometry, debris blockage, cover depth and local drainage criteria — normally following FHWA HDS-5. Anything under a roadway or beside a structure needs a licensed engineer.

Worked example: 5-acre suburban lot

Take a 5 acre drainage area with C = 0.35, a 3 in/hr design storm, a 1% slope and corrugated metal pipe (n = 0.024):

  1. Peak flow: 1.008 × 0.35 × 3 × 5 = 5.29 cfs (2,376 gpm, 0.150 m³/s).
  2. With an 80% capacity limit, the pipe must carry at least 6.62 cfs flowing full.
  3. Manning's equation gives a minimum inside diameter of 19.05 in (484 mm).
  4. Round up to the next standard size: 24 in / 600 mm. Its full-flow capacity is 12.25 cfs, so the design storm uses only 43% of capacity.
  5. Full-flow velocity is 3.90 ft/s — above the 2 ft/s sediment threshold and well below the 10 ft/s scour threshold.

Choosing the inputs

  • Runoff coefficient C — roofs and pavement shed almost everything (0.70–0.95); lawns on sandy flat ground absorb most of it (0.10–0.20). Mixed suburban lots usually land near 0.30–0.40.
  • Rainfall intensity — read from a local IDF curve for your design return period (often 10 or 25 years for a driveway culvert) at the storm duration equal to Tc.
  • Slope — match the existing channel where possible; 0.5–2% is the usual working range.
  • Manning n — rougher pipe means less capacity. Compare CMP with concrete or HDPE in the calculator before you buy.
  • Capacity limit — 80% leaves room for sediment, debris and a slightly imperfect grade. Use 70% where blockage is likely.
  • Call before you dig. In the US dial 811 to have buried utilities marked — it's free and it's the law.

Frequently asked questions

What size culvert do I need?

First estimate peak flow with the Rational Method (Q = C × i × A), then use Manning's equation to find the inside diameter whose full-flow capacity exceeds that flow with a safety margin, and round up to a standard pipe size. For example, 5 acres with C = 0.35 and a 3 in/hr storm gives 5.29 cfs, which needs about 19 in inside diameter at a 1% slope — a 24 in (600 mm) culvert is the standard choice.

What is the Rational Method formula?

Q = C × i × A, where C is the runoff coefficient, i is rainfall intensity for a storm duration equal to the time of concentration, and A is the drainage area. In US units Q (cfs) = 1.008 × C × i (in/hr) × A (acres); the 1.008 factor is often rounded to 1. In SI units Q (m³/s) = C × i (mm/hr) × A (hectares) ÷ 360. It is intended for small drainage areas, typically under 20 acres.

What slope should a culvert have?

Start at 1% (1 ft of fall per 100 ft) and keep the pipe close to the natural channel grade. Below roughly 0.5% sediment tends to settle in the barrel; above 2–3% outlet velocity rises and you may need riprap or an energy dissipator. Always check the fall with a pipe slope calculation before you set the invert.

What Manning's n should I use for a culvert?

Common values are 0.024 for corrugated metal pipe (CMP), 0.027 for older or larger corrugated steel, 0.013 for concrete pipe and 0.012 for smooth HDPE. Rougher, older or sediment-coated pipe has a higher n and less capacity. This calculator lets you choose the material, so you can compare them directly.

Is this a final culvert design?

No. This is a planning-level barrel size based on open-channel full-flow capacity. A real design also checks inlet control and outlet control headwater, tailwater, barrel length, inlet geometry, debris blockage, cover depth and local drainage criteria, normally following FHWA HDS-5. Have a licensed engineer sign off on anything under a roadway or near a structure.
