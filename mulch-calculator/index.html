How this calculator works

The volume math is simple — the useful part is the cost comparison between bagged and bulk delivery:

volume (ft³) = length × width × (depth ÷ 12) cubic yards = volume ÷ 27 2 cf bags = volume ÷ 2 3 cf bags = volume ÷ 3 bags cost = 2 cf bags × bag price bulk cost = cubic yards × bulk price per yard
volume (m³) = length × width × (depth ÷ 100) bulk cost = volume × bulk price per m³
⚠️ Estimates only. Bulk mulch is sold by the yard with delivery minimums that vary by supplier; bag prices vary by brand and season. Add one extra bag (or round the yardage up) — mulch settles and you will touch up bare spots by fall.

Spreading tips

  • 2–3 inches is the sweet spot. Thicker invites rot and rodents; thinner lets weeds through.
  • Keep it off the stems. Pull mulch 3–4 inches back from trunks — "volcano mulching" kills trees slowly.
  • Refresh every spring. Mulch breaks down into soil food; add ~1 inch per year rather than re-spreading full depth.
  • Skip the fabric under mulch in beds — a proper mulch layer does the weed job and feeds the soil as it decomposes.

Frequently asked questions

How deep should mulch be?

Two to three inches is right for most beds: enough to hold moisture and block weeds without suffocating roots. Never exceed four inches, and keep mulch pulled back a few inches from tree trunks and plant stems to prevent rot.

How many 2 cubic feet bags are in a cubic yard of mulch?

One cubic yard equals 27 cubic feet, so it takes 13.5 two-cubic-foot bags — round up to 14. If you buy 3-cubic-foot bags, one cubic yard is exactly 9 bags.

How much area does a cubic yard of mulch cover?

At the standard 3-inch depth, one cubic yard covers about 108 square feet. At 2 inches it covers about 160 square feet, and at 4 inches about 80 square feet. This calculator does the exact math for your bed size.

Is it cheaper to buy mulch in bags or bulk?

Bulk mulch by the cubic yard is typically 30 to 50 percent cheaper than the same amount in bags, but most suppliers deliver 5-yard minimums or charge delivery. Under about 3 cubic yards, bags are usually more practical; beyond that, bulk wins on price. This calculator compares both for your project.

Should I use landscape fabric under mulch?

Usually not. Fabric blocks the organic matter that feeds soil, mulch slides around on top of it, and weeds root into the fabric layer anyway. A 3-inch layer of mulch alone suppresses most weeds, and replenishing it each spring feeds the soil as it breaks down.
