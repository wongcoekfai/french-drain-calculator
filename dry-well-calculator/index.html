How this calculator works

The sizing method is the standard rational estimate used for residential roof dispersion:

runoff (gal) = roof area (ft²) × rain (in) ÷ 12 × 7.48 storage needed = runoff ÷ void ratio (gravel pit ≈ 0.35) diameter (ft) = √( 4 × storage (ft³) ÷ (π × depth) )
runoff (liters) = roof area (m²) × rain (mm) ÷ 1000 × 1000 storage needed = runoff ÷ void ratio (gravel pit ≈ 0.35) diameter (m) = √( 4 × storage (m³) ÷ (π × depth) )
⚠️ Estimates only. A dry well only works if surrounding soil can drain — do a simple percolation test first. Keep wells at least 10 ft from foundations, above the seasonal water table, and check local stormwater rules (some areas regulate discharge).

Installation tips

  • Line it. Wrap the pit in non-woven geotextile and add a sediment trap or leaf screen at the inlet — silt is what kills dry wells.
  • Overflow path. Give the well a controlled overflow (second pipe to daylight or a second well) for storms bigger than your design storm.
  • Dig safely. Call 811 before digging — it's free and it's the law in the US.
  • Route the pipe with slope. Use the pipe slope calculator to figure the drop from downspout to well.

Frequently asked questions

What size dry well do I need?

A common rule of thumb is 1 gallon of storage per square foot of roof per 1 inch of rain, then size the dry well to hold that volume. For example, a 1,000 sq ft roof with a 2-inch design storm needs about 2,000 gallons of effective storage, which a 6 ft diameter by 6 ft deep gravel dry well (with ~35% void space) roughly provides.

How deep should a dry well be?

Most residential dry wells are 3 to 8 feet deep and 3 to 6 feet in diameter. Keep the bottom at least 10 feet from foundations and above the seasonal water table, and check that the surrounding soil drains — a percolation test is worth doing before you dig.

How much runoff does my roof produce?

Roof runoff in gallons equals roof area (sq ft) × rainfall depth (inches) × 0.623 ÷ 12 × 7.48. Since 1 inch of rain on 1,000 sq ft produces about 620 gallons, roofs generate far more water than people expect — which is why undersized dry wells overflow in the first big storm.

Do dry wells need to be maintained?

Yes, minimally. Install a filter fabric wrapper and a sediment trap or leaf screen upstream, and inspect the inlet yearly. A silted-up dry well is the most common failure; clean fabric or replace the top layer of gravel every few years to keep infiltration rates up.

Is a gravel dry well or a prefabricated chamber better?

A gravel pit is cheapest but only about 30–40% of its volume stores water (the rest is stone). Prefabricated dry well basins and modular chambers store nearly 100% of their volume, so they are much smaller for the same capacity — often worth the extra cost where digging space is limited.
