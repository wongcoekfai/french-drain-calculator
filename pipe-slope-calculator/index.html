How this calculator works

Slope is simply vertical drop divided by horizontal run. The calculator converts the plumber's "inches per foot" notation into percent and gives you the depth to dig at the far end:

slope % = (fall per foot ÷ 12) × 100 total drop = fall per foot × run length end depth = start depth + total drop angle = arctan(slope % ÷ 100)
slope % = (fall per meter ÷ 100) × 100 = fall per meter × 100 total drop = fall per meter × run length end depth = start depth + total drop angle = arctan(slope % ÷ 100)
⚠️ Estimates for planning only. Follow your local plumbing code — minimum slopes vary by pipe size and jurisdiction (IPC/UPC differ in places). Never slope so little that water sits, or so much that solids get left behind.

Slope rules of thumb

  • 3 inch pipe: 1/4 inch per foot (≈2%) — the household standard.
  • 4 inch and larger: 1/8 inch per foot (≈1%) is the code minimum.
  • French drain perforated pipe: 1 inch of drop per 8–10 feet works well; see the french drain calculator for the full trench design.
  • Too flat (<0.5%) lets solids settle; too steep (>4–5%) lets water outrun solids — both end in clogs.
  • Call before you dig. In the US dial 811 to have buried utilities marked — it's free and it's the law.

Frequently asked questions

What slope should a drainage pipe have?

The standard slope for 3 to 4 inch drainage and sewer pipe is 1/4 inch per foot, which equals about a 2% grade. Larger pipes can use less: a 6 inch pipe needs at least 1/8 inch per foot (about 1%). The goal is water fast enough to carry solids but not so fast that water outruns them.

How do you calculate pipe slope?

Slope equals the vertical drop divided by the horizontal run. For example, a pipe that falls 1 foot over a 50 foot run has a slope of 1/50 = 0.02 = 2%. In plumbing, slope is often expressed in inches of drop per foot of run, such as 1/4 inch per foot.

What is a 2% slope in inches per foot?

A 2% slope means the pipe drops 2 units for every 100 units of run, which is 0.24 inches per foot — in practice rounded to the standard 1/4 inch per foot used for household sewer and drain lines.

What is the minimum slope for a 4 inch drain pipe?

Most plumbing codes, including the IPC, require a minimum of 1/8 inch per foot (about 1%) for 4 inch and larger horizontal drainage pipe, and 1/4 inch per foot (about 2%) for 3 inch pipe. Always confirm with your local code.

Can a drain pipe be too steep?

Yes. If the slope exceeds about 1/2 inch per foot, water can outrun solid waste and leave it behind, eventually causing clogs. Very steep runs are usually solved with drop structures or re-routing rather than a single steep pitch.
