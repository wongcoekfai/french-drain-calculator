How this calculator works

The math is the same rational-method runoff used for gutter and drain sizing:

runoff (GPM) = 0.0104 × roof area (ft²) × rainfall intensity (in/hr)

One square foot of roof sheds about 0.62 gallons per hour for every inch of rainfall — multiply by your design storm intensity and the contributing roof area. The downspout capacity figures are approximate full-flow values from gutter manufacturer data; elbows, screens and partial clogs reduce them, so keep at least 25% headroom.

⚠️ Estimates only. Capacity figures assume straight, clean downspouts at full flow. If your result is within 25% of the limit, upsize the downspout or add another — overflowing gutters dump water against the foundation, which is far more expensive to fix than a bigger downspout.

Routing the water away

  • Minimum 6–10 ft from the foundation, discharging onto ground that slopes away from the house.
  • Splash blocks for small roofs and sandy soil; buried pipe to a dry well where soil drains slowly.
  • Tie into a French drain with a solid pipe — use our pipe slope calculator to keep it moving (1/8 to 1/4 inch per foot).
  • Size the receiving drain with the dry well calculator — one hour of the storm volumes above is the number to store.

Frequently asked questions

How many downspouts do I need per gutter?

A common rule is one 2×3 downspout for every 30 to 40 feet of gutter, but the honest answer depends on roof area and local rainfall. A 2×3 downspout handles roughly 500 to 700 square feet of roof in moderate climates; in heavy-rain regions you need a 3×4 downspout or more of them. This calculator sizes it from your actual roof area and design rainfall.

What is the difference between a 2×3 and 3×4 downspout?

A 3×4 downspout has roughly double the capacity of a 2×3 (about 45 GPM versus 23 GPM at full flow) and clogs far less often because leaves pass through the larger opening. If your calculator result is marginal on a 2×3, upsizing to 3×4 is cheap insurance compared with adding another downspout.

How much rain can a downspout handle?

Approximate full-flow capacities: a 2×3 inch rectangular downspout moves about 23 GPM, a 3×4 inch about 46 GPM, and a 4-inch round about 55 GPM. Real-world capacity drops if the run has elbows or partial clogs, so size with some headroom rather than at the exact limit.

Where should downspout water go?

Ideally at least 6 to 10 feet away from the foundation, on ground that slopes away from the house. Options in order of effort: splash blocks, above-ground extension pipes, buried corrugated or PVC pipe to a dry well, or a tie-in to a French drain. Dumping water at the foundation is the leading cause of basement moisture and foundation settling.

What rainfall intensity should I use?

Use your region's 10-minute storm intensity at roughly the 10-year return period: about 2 to 3 inches per hour in much of the northern US, 4 to 5 in the Midwest and mid-Atlantic, and 6 to 8 in the Gulf Coast. Your local building code or the NOAA Atlas 14 data gives the exact figure — the default of 4 in/hr is a sensible middle ground for most of the country.
